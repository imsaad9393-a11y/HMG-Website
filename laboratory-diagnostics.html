Routine laboratory testing

Hematology

What it is

Blood tests that look at blood cells, such as red cells, white cells and platelets.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor wants to check general health or investigate symptoms that may involve the blood.

What it cannot tell you

One result rarely gives a diagnosis on its own. Your doctor reads it together with your symptoms and other tests.

Biochemistry

What it is

Tests that measure chemicals and other substances in blood and body fluids.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor wants to check how the body is working or to follow a known condition.

What it cannot tell you

A single result cannot explain everything. Your doctor interprets it together with your health history and other tests.

Ask about Biochemistry

Serology

What it is

Tests that look for antibodies or antigens in the blood, which can show contact with certain infections.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor wants to check for an infection or for the body's response to one.

What it cannot tell you

Results depend on timing. A test may not show a very recent infection, and some antibodies stay in the blood after the illness has passed.

Special testing

Biopsy

What it is

Laboratory examination of tissue samples under a microscope.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor has taken a tissue sample and needs it examined to help reach a diagnosis.

What it cannot tell you

The laboratory result needs medical context. Your doctor reads it together with your examination and other tests.

Ask about Biopsy

Special chemistry

What it is

Specialised chemical tests that go beyond routine panels.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor asks for a more detailed test after routine results, or when symptoms need a closer look.

What it cannot tell you

A single result seldom tells the whole story. Your doctor decides what it means for you.

Ask about Special chemistry

Microbiology

What it is

Tests that look for bacteria, fungi and other microorganisms in a sample.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor suspects an infection and wants to identify what is causing it.

What it cannot tell you

Results depend on how and when the sample was taken, and a negative result does not always rule out an infection.

Ask about Microbiology

PCR

What it is

Molecular tests that detect specific genetic material, for example from a germ or a gene, by making many copies of a tiny amount.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor wants to look for a specific infection or genetic change.

What it cannot tell you

It only finds what the test was designed to look for, and a negative result does not always rule out a problem.
