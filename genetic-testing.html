Exome sequencing

What it is

Reads the parts of a person's genes that carry instructions for making proteins, to look for changes linked to genetic conditions.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor suspects a genetic condition that simpler tests have not explained.

What it cannot tell you

It may not find a cause. It does not detect every type of genetic change, and some results are uncertain and need expert interpretation.

Genome sequencing

What it is

Reads most of a person's DNA, including parts outside the genes, to look for genetic changes.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor suspects a genetic condition and wants a broader look than a targeted test.

What it cannot tell you

It does not guarantee an answer. Some changes found have an uncertain meaning, and results need expert interpretation alongside your medical history.

NIPT

What it is

A screening test during pregnancy. It analyses small pieces of DNA in the mother's blood to estimate the chance of certain chromosome conditions in the baby.

Who may ask about it

Pregnant women who want to know more about these chances, usually after talking with their doctor.

What it cannot tell you

It does not give a diagnosis. A higher-chance result is normally followed by a diagnostic test, and a lower-chance result does not rule out every condition.

Ask about NIPT

SMA testing

What it is

Testing related to spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), an inherited condition that affects the nerves that control muscles.

Who may ask about it

People with symptoms their doctor links to SMA, families with a history of SMA, and people who want to know whether they carry the condition.

What it cannot tell you

It cannot predict exactly how the condition will affect a person. A result is best discussed with a doctor or genetic counsellor.

Karyotyping

What it is

Looks at a person's chromosomes under a microscope to check their number and overall structure.

Who may ask about it

People whose doctor wants to check for chromosome changes, for example in a child's development or in fertility and pregnancy concerns.

What it cannot tell you

It cannot see small changes inside individual genes. Other tests are needed for those.

Ask about Karyotyping

PGD

What it is

Genetic testing of embryos created through assisted reproduction (IVF), done before an embryo is transferred.

Who may ask about it

Couples who carry a known genetic condition or chromosome change and want to understand their options with their fertility doctor.

What it cannot tell you

It does not guarantee a pregnancy or a healthy baby, and it only tests for the conditions it is set up to look for. It involves IVF and is best discussed in counselling first.
